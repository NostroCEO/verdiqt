# Demand Signal Hierarchy

## The Five Rungs, Ranked by Cost of the Action

Rank every piece of evidence by the costliest thing the person has already done, not by how loudly they talk. From strongest to weakest: purchases, active seeking, complaints, interest, opinions. A purchase means money already moves toward a solution: a competitor subscription, a contractor, a duct-taped stack of three tools. Active seeking means the person spent time hunting for a fix: comparison searches, "any tool that does X" threads, trial signups. A complaint describes pain with no hunt attached. Interest is a costless click: upvotes, follows, waitlist emails. An opinion costs nothing and predicts nothing: "someone should build this." The heuristic for scoring: severity is claimed in words, willingness to pay is proven in actions, and only the top two rungs prove anything.

## How Each Rung Appears in Forums, Search, and Reviews

In forum posts, read for verbs and dollar figures. "We pay 400 a month for X and still export to spreadsheets by hand" is purchase-rung evidence with a visible wedge. "Recommendations for something that handles X?" is active seeking. A rant thread with no request in it is a complaint. A pile of upvotes on someone else's rant is interest, one rung lower than the rant itself. In search behavior, "X pricing," "X vs Y," and "alternative to X" sit on the seeking rung and imply budget; "how to fix X" and "X not working" are complaints; "what is X" is interest at best. Product reviews start on the purchase rung by definition, so read them for churn intent instead: "canceled because," "switching to," and 2-star reviews from long-term customers mark the sharpest wedges. A 5-star review that ends with a feature wish maps an underserved segment inside a proven paying channel.

## Weak Signals Do Not Convert Into Strong Ones

There is no exchange rate. A thousand upvotes do not equal one paid invoice, and ten thousand do not either, because the rungs measure different things: lower rungs measure attention, upper rungs measure willingness to pay. Aggregation is only valid within a rung. Fifty independent complaints about the same workflow, from different people across different channels, is a strong severity reading. It still says nothing about whether anyone will pay, and severity without willingness to pay describes a content niche, not a SaaS. The scoring rule: a candidate whose best evidence sits on the interest or opinion rung caps out at a weak score no matter the volume. Volume widens confidence within a rung; it never promotes evidence up a rung. The only thing that promotes evidence is a costlier observed action.

## Stale Signals Decay, and Decay Is Not Linear

Demand evidence rots because markets self-heal: incumbents ship the missing feature, a free tool absorbs the use case, the channel that surfaced the complaint dies. Weight signals by age. Under 6 months old: full weight. Between 6 and 18 months: half weight, and check whether the named incumbent has since shipped a fix before scoring at all. Past 18 months: near zero unless you can re-confirm it with a fresh instance of the same signal. Decay hits the lower rungs hardest. An old opinion is worthless; an old purchase pattern often survives, because budgets and switching costs move slowly. Chronic pain is the exception that ages well: a complaint that keeps reappearing across years, unresolved, signals a structural gap incumbents cannot or will not close. One recent strong signal beats an archive of stale weak ones, and a stale purchase signal still outranks a fresh opinion.
