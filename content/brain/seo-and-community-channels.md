# Reading Channel Evidence

A distribution score is not a bet on future marketing skill. It is a read on whether demand already moves through channels you can reach today. Four evidence types matter: search gaps, community density, directory rails, and borrowed audiences.

## Search-Gap Signals: Weak Answers Beat Big Volume

The strongest search evidence is high intent meeting poor supply, not raw volume. Pull the top ten results for a commercial-intent query in the niche. If three or more are forum threads, Q&A scraps, or posts older than two years, you are looking at a gap: people are searching and nobody has answered well. A volume floor still applies: a head query in the 100 to 1,000 monthly range, surrounded by a cluster of 20 or more long-tail variants, indicates a durable pattern rather than a spike. Query language maps directly to scoring dimensions. "How do I fix" and "workaround for" phrasing signals problem severity. Queries containing "pricing," "alternative," or "vs" signal willingness to pay, because nobody comparison-shops for a problem they will not spend on. A niche with severity queries but zero commercial queries deserves a lower score: pain exists, budget may not.

## Community Density: Few Live Hubs Beat Many Dead Ones

Count hubs, then measure pulse, and only the pulse counts. One subreddit with 40,000 members and 20 new threads a day outweighs ten Slack groups that went quiet last year. Score density on the top three hubs only. Each should clear roughly 5,000 members with visible activity: 10 or more new threads per week and replies landing within 24 hours. A graveyard of dead communities is negative evidence, not neutral: the audience exists but does not congregate, which raises acquisition cost for every channel you try later. Inside live hubs, look for two recurring artifacts. Complaint threads about incumbent tools are wedge material. A "what do you all use for X" thread appearing monthly means the community actively routes buying decisions, which is the single best free channel signal available.

## Directories and Marketplaces as Distribution Rails

App stores, plugin marketplaces, integration directories, and review platforms are rails: standing demand routed by category search, no audience-building required. Check three things. Does a category exist that matches the wedge? Do the top listings show real traction, meaning the top three carry 500 or more reviews or visible install counts, which proves buyers spend through this rail? Is there a quality gap, meaning leaders sit at 4.2 stars or below with complaints clustering on one recurring flaw? A live category with a clustered flaw is a strong entry signal. An empty category is ambiguous: it is either an opening or a demand vacuum, and search-gap data is the tiebreaker. Rails also shape retention math: users acquired through a marketplace arrive pre-qualified by category intent and typically churn less than cold traffic, so rail presence should lift a distribution score more than an equivalent volume of paid clicks.

## Borrowed-Audience Plays

At validation stage, renting attention beats building it. Inventory the newsletters, podcasts, and YouTube channels serving the target buyer. Five or more reachable audiences above 10,000 subscribers, with visible sponsorship slots or guest openings, means the channel is rentable and message testing is cheap. The test itself has a bar: one sponsorship or guest appearance that lands 100 or more clicks converting to signups above 3 percent validates message-channel fit before any paid spend. The inverse finding matters as much: if no borrowable audience exists for a supposed niche, question whether the niche exists at all. When search gaps, live hubs, and a rail converge with rentable audiences, distribution risk is low and the score should say so, whatever the other dimensions read.
